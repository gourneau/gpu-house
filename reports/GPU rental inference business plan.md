# Rent one Blackwell node, serve list-price models, stay flexible

Josh should not start a general "rent GPUs and resell them" business, and he should not buy servers for the deduction. One narrow configuration pencils out: an 8-GPU NVIDIA B200 node rented from a provider whose terms allow a public paid API (Vultr at $3.50 per GPU-hour, checked 2026-10-22), serving the two or three large open-weight models whose OpenRouter hosts all charge the model lab's list price (StepFun Step 5 Preview at $1.00 in / $2.70 out and Tencent Hy4 preview at $0.834 / $2.501 per million tokens), listed on OpenRouter at 5 percent under that floor. The deterministic model puts conservative revenue for that setup at **$23.39 per B200-hour at 100 percent utilization against a $3.50 rental cost**, a breakeven utilization of 15 percent, and a 12-month after-tax result of **+$54,607 at 40 percent utilization on a $100,000 budget** (model output, `data/results.md`, inputs in `data/params_notes.md`). Every H100 configuration loses money at every utilization because the models that fit one H100 have price floors near zero ($0.03 / $0.15 per million for gpt-oss-120b) and 10 to 22 hosts already. The plan has one gate Josh does not control: OpenRouter is the only USD per-token marketplace that pays independents, it reports "a large backlog" and prioritizes "proprietary models," and one secondhand account puts acceptance near 1 in 40 applications per day. So the realistic December 2026 paying customer is a direct one (a dedicated endpoint or a managed-inference engagement), with OpenRouter as the upside. A 12-month prepayment made in December with service starting in December is deductible in 2026 under the 12-month rule (Reg. 1.263(a)-4(f)) and Zaninovich, but only if the business is open to customers by December 31, the payment is real and non-refundable, and total business losses stay under the $256,000 excess-business-loss cap he shares with any camper-van purchase. The tax saving is 49.3 cents per dollar; it softens a loss, it does not create a profit. Crypto and token-paid networks are listed and flagged; nothing in them is profitable on rented GPUs today.

## Only a handful of clouds sell cheap GPUs and allow a public paid API

The cheapest GPU is not the cheapest usable GPU. The research read the terms of 30 providers and sorted them into three groups: terms that permit or contemplate third-party end users, terms that forbid resale or "service bureau" use, and terms never read. Only the first group is priced into the model. **Vultr is the price leader for H100, B200, MI300X and MI355X, Koyeb for H200 and RTX PRO 6000, and both permit a paid API**; DigitalOcean is the only provider whose terms name "resale" and "your own products and services" as allowed uses, which makes it the lowest-legal-risk source even where it is not the cheapest ([Vultr pricing](https://www.vultr.com/pricing/); [Koyeb pricing](https://www.koyeb.com/pricing); [DigitalOcean ToS 4.4](https://www.digitalocean.com/legal/terms-of-service-agreement); checked 2026-10-22). Vultr sells these GPUs only as 8-GPU plans, publishes no stock indicator, and gates new accounts behind a manually reviewed account limit that two October 2026 Trustpilot reviews describe as a "vague, contradictory approval gauntlet" with "an arbitrary 30-day 'normal usage' wait" ([Vultr docs](https://docs.vultr.com/support/platform/billing/how-can-i-increase-my-account-limits); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/vultr.com); checked 2026-10-22). That is why the staged scenarios below test on a single Koyeb GPU first.

Three things about term pricing matter for the December deduction. First, almost nobody publishes a 12-month price: DigitalOcean does (H100 $3.26, H200 $3.40, MI300X $1.91, 12-month contractual commitment via sales), Verda does (2 percent off at 1 month, 3 percent at 3 months, 8 percent at 1 year), Jarvislabs does (2, 3, 5 and 9 percent), and Vultr prints bare-metal "starting from" prices "for pre-paid contract terms" with no term stated ([DigitalOcean](https://www.digitalocean.com/pricing/gpu-droplets); [Verda](https://verda.com/pricing); [Jarvislabs](https://jarvislabs.ai/pricing); [Vultr](https://www.vultr.com/pricing/); checked 2026-10-22). Second, where a reserved price is published it often sits above on-demand: Lambda's 1-Click Cluster H100 is $6.16 per GPU-hour at a 16-GPU minimum against $3.99 on-demand for 8x, so reservation there buys guaranteed InfiniBand capacity, not a discount ([Lambda 1-Click Clusters](https://lambda.ai/1-click-clusters); checked 2026-10-22). Third, the only self-serve prepay with a published discount mechanic is Vast's reserved instance (CLI default discount 0.2 for 1 month, 0.3 for 3 months, credits locked to one host, early cancel claws back the discount plus 5 percent) and RunPod's 3- or 6-month savings plan with no published percentage ([Vast reserved](https://docs.vast.ai/documentation/instances/choosing/reserved-instances); [RunPod pods pricing](https://docs.runpod.io/pods/pricing); checked 2026-10-22). So the "12-month prepaid" scenarios model the tax timing of a prepayment at roughly the on-demand rate; any negotiated discount is upside.

## OpenRouter is the only USD marketplace that pays independents, and its door is narrow

The research looked for every place a one-person operator can list an OpenAI-compatible endpoint and get paid per token. Four doors exist for USD, and three of them are nearly shut. **OpenRouter** accepts outside providers by application, pays "through monthly invoicing" in USD, takes no markup on inference (its fee is 5.5 percent on the buyer's credit purchases), and routes traffic by price and speed ([OpenRouter providers page](https://openrouter.ai/providers/apply); [OpenRouter pricing](https://openrouter.ai/pricing); checked 2026-10-22). It also says: "We currently have a large backlog of provider applications and are prioritizing providers with proprietary models," and "Due to high demand, not all providers will be accepted" ([OpenRouter providers page](https://openrouter.ai/providers/apply); checked 2026-10-22). One secondhand report of an August 20, 2026 OpenRouter event says the team "receives about 40 provider applications each day and accepts about one" and that "legal review of providers is the current bottleneck" ([dylanvu.substack.com](https://dylanvu.substack.com/p/how-to-get-listed-on-openrouter-as); published 2026-08-21, checked 2026-10-22). No approval time, net payment term, minimum payout or payout rail is published anywhere; the research found no first-person account of a solo provider being listed. Stripe agreed to buy OpenRouter on August 19, 2026, and the company says its product and roadmap continue ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/News/2026/08/OpenRouter-to-be-Acquired-by-Stripe-to-Help-Businesses-Optimize-Token-Routing-and-Usage); checked 2026-10-22).

**Hugging Face Inference Providers** publishes a nine-step self-serve path (OpenAI-compatible API, a huggingface.js pull request, a billing endpoint in nano-USD) but step 3 requires HF to enable the organization server-side on a paid Team or Enterprise plan, HF publishes no revenue share or payout terms, and its public feedback thread has 96 posts dated 2026 and no staff reply since September 23, 2025, including unanswered October 2026 applicants running "one RTX 5090" and "a dedicated GPU server in Helsinki" ([HF register as a provider](https://huggingface.co/docs/inference-providers/en/register-as-a-provider); [HF discussion #49](https://huggingface.co/spaces/huggingface/HuggingDiscussions/discussions/49); checked 2026-10-22). **NanoGPT** takes providers by email but says "A single-GPU or single-operator service is unlikely to be a fit unless it offers something genuinely unique" ([NanoGPT for providers](https://docs.nano-gpt.com/api-reference/miscellaneous/for-providers); checked 2026-10-22). **cheaperinference.com** buys cloud API quota (AWS, Microsoft, Google, Venice accounts) through a seller's API key, not GPU capacity, and shifts all cloud-terms risk to the seller ([Seller Agreement](https://platform.cheaperinference.com/legal/seller-agreement); checked 2026-10-22). Everything else in the category is either a buyer's gateway with no sell side (Vercel AI Gateway, Cloudflare AI Gateway, Portkey, Helicone, LiteLLM, Requesty, Eden AI), a first-party cloud (DeepInfra, Fireworks, Baseten, Novita, Parasail), dead (Kluster.ai shut inference in July 2025; Unify and Martian pivoted; cheapinference.com is a parked domain), or crypto-paid (Chutes, Targon, Gonka), which all now require TEE-capable bare metal or 320 GB-plus VRAM nodes and exclude RunPod and Vast pods ([inference_marketplaces notes, cited per row](https://chutes.ai/docs/miner-resources/overview); checked 2026-10-22).

What OpenRouter requires once inside is concrete. The endpoint must be OpenAI-compatible with streaming and usage tokens, publish a `/models` feed with USD prices and datacenter locations, support monthly invoicing, and have "a published privacy policy and clear data retention terms" that disclose logging and training ([OpenRouter providers page](https://openrouter.ai/providers/apply); checked 2026-10-22). Uptime is measured as successful requests divided by total requests after 100 requests: "95%+ uptime" keeps normal routing, 80 to 94 percent reduces traffic, under 80 percent is fallback only ([OpenRouter integration docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/providers/integration); checked 2026-10-22). Default routing load-balances "weighted by inverse square of the price" among stable providers, so a $1 endpoint is "9x more likely" to be picked than a $3 one, but the newer provider-facing page says share depends on "your adjusted price and your speed," that "past the edge, your share drops off fast," and that "Every endpoint still gets a small amount of traffic no matter what" ([OpenRouter provider selection](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection); [OpenRouter traffic routing](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/providers/traffic-routing); checked 2026-10-22). Being the cheapest does not win: on gpt-oss-120b the two $0.03 / $0.15 floor endpoints carried 0.8 percent and 7.2 percent of the day's tokens while a $0.09 / $0.36 endpoint carried 24.5 percent, and the fastest providers (Cerebras 806 tok/s, SambaNova 333, Groq 313) charge 3 to 10 times the floor ([OpenRouter gpt-oss-120b page](https://openrouter.ai/openai/gpt-oss-120b); checked 2026-10-22). The model's 7 percent routing-share assumption for a new entrant comes from that page and is an estimate.

The volume is real and concentrated. OpenRouter carries roughly 21.9 trillion tokens a day; the weekly leaderboard through October 21, 2026 puts DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at 25.7T tokens (down 31 percent week over week), its 1017 snapshot at 16.5T, GLM 5.3 Flash at 11.3T, Hy4 preview at 8.55T (up 9 percent), Step 5 Preview at 2.32T (down 88 percent), and Kimi K3 at 1.72T ([OpenRouter rankings](https://openrouter.ai/rankings); [Blockworks](https://x.com/BlockworksAdv/article/2110415412580020472); checked 2026-10-22). The models that fit one H100 are long-tail: gpt-oss-120b 568B tokens a week across 22 providers, Llama 3.3 70B 39.1B across 10, Qwen3 32B 13.3B across 3 ([OpenRouter models listing](https://openrouter.ai/models?order=top-weekly); checked 2026-10-22). ## Rented GPUs cannot be relisted; only owned hardware can, and the spread is thin

The "rent cheap, relist on Vast" idea fails on contract before it fails on math. Every renter-side agreement read (Lambda, Nebius, Crusoe, Voltage Park, Hyperbolic, TensorDock, Vast's own terms) prohibits reselling, renting or leasing the service to third parties, and on the host side Vast's Standard Hosting Agreement makes the provider warrant that "it is the sole owner of the Hardware" and that "The Hardware shall at all times be located at the Provider" ([Vast Standard Hosting Agreement](https://cloud.vast.ai/host/agreement); [Lambda ToS](https://lambda.ai/legal/terms-of-service); [Nebius ToU 2.8](https://docs.nebius.com/legal/terms-of-use); checked 2026-10-22). Technically the Vast install script has no hypervisor check, so a root VM from Lambda, Nebius or Crusoe could run the Docker-only host daemon, and a RunPod pod cannot because "you cannot spin up your own Docker instance" on Pods; but the daemon needs a public IPv4 with 5 or more forwarded ports per GPU, 500 Mbps up and down, a dedicated 200 GB Docker partition and root, and unverified hosts are filtered out of search by default ([Vast install script](https://s3.amazonaws.com/vast.ai/install); [Vast verification stages](https://docs.vast.ai/host/verification-stages.md); [RunPod Pods overview](https://docs.runpod.io/pods/overview); checked 2026-10-22). RunPod closed its Community Cloud host program to new hosts on February 24, 2026; TensorDock takes a 25 percent fee (its agreement text literally says "twenty percent (25%)"), pays via Stripe above a $250 balance, and requires hosts to run its Libvirt hypervisor in a data center ([RunPod blog via Plushcap](https://www.plushcap.com/companies/runpod/blog/summaries/2026/2); [TensorDock host page](https://www.tensordock.com/host.html); [TensorDock Supplier Agreement](https://docs.tensordock.com/legal-information/supplier-hosting-agreement); checked 2026-10-22). The one contractually sanctioned secondary market is SF Compute, where a buyer "may resell an unused portion of an Accepted Contract on the Marketplace" with "prior authorization from the Company," for platform credits rather than cash, under a two-part fee that is "set by SF Compute" and unpublished; SF Compute reports its customers realize "an average 25% realized discount" that way, which is a recovery on idle time, not a profit ([SF Compute ToS 3.4](https://sfcompute.com/legal/terms-of-service); [SF Compute reselling guide](https://docs.sfcompute.com/preview/guides/reselling.md); [SF Compute news](https://sfcompute.com/news/realized-discount); checked 2026-10-22). Eight targeted searches found no public account of anyone renting a cloud GPU and relisting it anywhere.

The spread on owned hardware is small and Vast does not publish the number that decides it. Vast's blog says "Live GPU prices are typically about 25% above what hosts earn," which implies the host keeps about 80 percent; third-party sites claim 85 to 90 percent; the research treated 20 percent as the fee ([Vast blog](https://vast.ai/article/how-much-money-can-you-earn-renting-out-your-gpu-on-vast-ai); checked 2026-10-22). Vast's own calculator calls utilization "your assumption, not a marketplace measurement" and defaults to 65 percent; the model uses a 50 percent idle fill rate (estimate) ([Vast host calculator](https://vast.ai/hosting/calculator); checked 2026-10-22). Payouts are weekly via Wise, PayPal or Stripe above a $20 balance, roughly two weeks after the billing week ([Vast host payouts](https://docs.vast.ai/host/payment.md); checked 2026-10-22).

## Unit economics: list-price models on B200 pay; everything on one H100 loses

The model computes revenue per GPU-hour as output tokens per second per GPU times 3,600, times (output price plus 3 times input price), times a 0.8 prefill haircut, at the "price to beat" (floor minus 5 percent). Throughput per GPU is the "conservative" column of the throughput notes (mixed real traffic at about 50 tokens per second per user with a general-purpose engine, about one third of the vendor offline figure for single-GPU models and one quarter for 8-GPU MoE models) or the "typical" column (tuned engine, 30 to 50 tok/s per user, about half to two thirds of vendor offline) ([throughput notes, planning table](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/latest/developer-guide/perf-overview.html); `data/params_notes.md` section 4; checked 2026-10-22). Two patterns drive everything downstream. First, price matters more than speed: Step 5 and Hy4 earn $20 to $23 per B200-hour because their output price is $2.38 to $2.57 per million and their input price is above $0.79, while DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, the highest-volume model on the platform, earns $1.60 per B200-hour because 29 hosts have pushed its floor to $0.07 / $0.18. Second, the B200 and MI355X multipliers (3.8 to 4.9 times H200 on large MoE at FP4 per InferenceX) turn the same model from a loss on H200 into a 15 to 20 percent breakeven on Blackwell ([InferenceX compare](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/run/deepseek-r1-on-b200); checked 2026-10-22). Three sanity checks on those big numbers. InferenceX's own retail-tier cost for DeepSeek R1 on B200 at 50 tok/s per user is $0.22 per million tokens at $3.70 per GPU-hour, which is the same arithmetic as the model's $14.98 per B200-hour for R1 at a $2.04 output price ([InferenceX R1 B200](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/run/deepseek-r1-on-b200); checked 2026-10-22). And the 7 percent routing share is an estimate from one model page; OpenRouter's own page says a new endpoint "starts out looking like a typical peer" and that "past the edge, your share drops off fast," so the first-month share may be lower ([OpenRouter traffic routing](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/providers/traffic-routing); checked 2026-10-22).

## Tax: a December prepayment is deductible if the business is open and the payment is real

The tax mechanics favor the plan in a narrow way and punish two obvious shortcuts. A cash-basis sole proprietor deducts cloud bills "for the taxable year in which paid," so December 2026 usage paid in December is a 2026 deduction and December usage paid in January is a 2027 deduction ([26 CFR 1.461-1(a)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.461-1); [IRS Pub 538](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p538); checked 2026-10-22). A prepayment is deductible when paid only under the 12-month rule: the benefit must not extend beyond the earlier of "12 months after the first date on which the taxpayer realizes the right or benefit" or "the end of the taxable year following the taxable year in which the payment is made" ([26 CFR 1.263(a)-4(f)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subject-group-ECFR9b4e8d8fb4d5b2b/section-1.263(a)-4); checked 2026-10-22). The regulation's own Example 2 (a premium paid December 1 for a policy starting December 15 and running 12 months) passes, and Example 1 (the same payment for a policy starting February 1) fails and must be capitalized. The Ninth Circuit, which covers California, held in Zaninovich that a cash-basis taxpayer's December 20 payment for a lease year running December 1 to November 30 "was fully deductible in the year of payment," and that ordinary cash-method mismatch is not a distortion of income ([Zaninovich v. Commissioner, 616 F.2d 429](https://openjurist.org/616/f2d/429); checked 2026-10-22). Applied to the scenarios: a 12-month (or shorter) GPU reservation paid in full and non-refundable in December 2026 with service starting in December 2026 or on January 1, 2027 is deductible in 2026; a reservation whose service starts January 15, 2027 and runs 12 months fails the rule; a contract for a fixed block of GPU-hours rather than a fixed period, or one with "a reasonable expectancy of renewal," can fall outside the rule under (f)(4) and (f)(5) ([26 CFR 1.263(a)-4(f)(4), (f)(5)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subject-group-ECFR9b4e8d8fb4d5b2b/section-1.263(a)-4); checked 2026-10-22). Write the Vultr or Koyeb contract as a fixed term of N months starting on a stated December date, paid at signing, no refund, no auto-renewal. The notes found no IRS ruling specific to prepaid cloud compute; Reg. 1.263(a)-4(f)(7)'s mention of "non-renewable, 12-month service contracts" is the closest language.

The business must exist before the deduction does. Costs paid before the active trade or business begins are start-up expenditures under IRC 195: $5,000 deductible in the first year (reduced dollar for dollar above $50,000 of start-up costs) and the rest amortized over 180 months ([26 USC 195](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/195); checked 2026-10-22). In Kellett, the Tax Court held an online business began "when the website was open to the public," years before its first revenue, citing the rule that "a taxpayer's effort to sell goods or services may qualify as an active trade or business even if the taxpayer makes no sales" ([Grant Thornton on Kellett, T.C. Memo. 2022-62](https://www.grantthornton.com/library/newsletters/tax/2022/hot-topics/jul-05/tax-court-rules-on-online-business-start-up-expenses.aspx); checked 2026-10-22). So the API must be publicly open with a pricing page, signup and issued keys before December 31, 2026, and a December paying customer is the strongest possible evidence. If it is not open until January, a $100,000 December prepayment is recovered at roughly $6,667 a year over 15 years instead of in full in 2026 (estimate from the tax notes).

Owned hardware gets 100 percent federal bonus depreciation in the year it is placed in service, meaning "ready and available for a specific use," which a server racked, powered and able to serve by December 31 meets even with no customer yet; the One Big Beautiful Bill Act made the 100 percent rate permanent for property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025, and servers are 5-year "qualified technological equipment" ([26 USC 168(k)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/168); [IRS Pub 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946); [KPMG on Notice 2026-11](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/01/notice-2026-11-interim-guidance-100-percent-depreciation-deduction.html); checked 2026-10-22). California does not conform: it allows a $25,000 Section 179 election that phases out dollar for dollar above $200,000 of property placed in service, plus 5-year MACRS at 5 percent in year one under the mid-quarter convention that applies when everything goes into service in the fourth quarter ([R&TC 17255](https://california.public.law/codes/revenue_and_taxation_code_section_17255); [FTB 3885A instructions](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-3885a-instructions.html); [IRS Pub 946 Table A-5](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p946.pdf); checked 2026-10-22). That is why the model's owned rows show two deductible figures: a $100,000 server is $101,602 federal (with December colo) and $30,352 California, and the combined 2026 saving is about $40,700 versus $49,300 for $100,000 of rent, with the California shortfall recovered over 2027 to 2031 (model output; [tax notes Q11](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-540-tax-rate-schedules.pdf); checked 2026-10-22). A $250,000 hardware purchase trips the California phase-out: Section 179 falls to $0 at $225,000 of property, which the model does not apply, so the $250,000 owned rows overstate the California deduction slightly.

The $256,000 cap is binding at the top budget. For tax years beginning in 2026 the excess business loss threshold is $256,000 single and $512,000 joint; any excess "shall be treated as a net operating loss" carried forward, and the limit applies after the passive-loss rules ([Rev. Proc. 2025-32 section 4.31](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf); [26 USC 461(l)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/461); checked 2026-10-22). Camper-van purchases used in another business share the same cap. The model caps deductible 2026 spend at $256,000, which is why the staged $250,000 scenarios (6b at $284,991 to $300,587 of 2026 cash) save $127,340 to $129,516 rather than 49.3 percent of the cash. California's own 2026 threshold is unpublished (2025: $313,000 single) and its disallowed amount becomes a "carryover excess business loss," not an NOL ([FTB 3461 (2025)](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-3461.pdf); [R&TC 17560.5](https://california.public.law/codes/revenue_and_taxation_code_section_17560.5); checked 2026-10-22). If Josh also buys vans in 2026, the $100,000 GPU budget is the one that stays safely under the cap; $250,000 of GPUs plus vans will not.

The loss only offsets W-2 wages if Josh materially participates and is at risk. A solo operator who does the work satisfies the "substantially all of the participation" test with no hours floor, and the 100-hour-and-not-less-than-anyone-else test as a fallback; hours may be proven "by any reasonable means," so a weekly log from day one is enough ([26 CFR 1.469-5T(a), (f)(4)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.469-5T); checked 2026-10-22). Self-funded cash is at risk under 465(b)(1)(A), so Schedule C box 32a applies ([26 USC 465](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/465); checked 2026-10-22). Profit motive is the exposure a high-income W-2 earner invites: in Young (T.C. Memo. 2025-95) the court denied $2.9 million of ranch losses where the owners had "no general ledger," a business plan that appeared after the audit began, and $16 million of other income; in Huff (T.C. Memo. 2021-140) a wealthy owner kept losses because the activity had its own books, bank account and an unwritten but real plan, and was "still in its startup stage" ([Journal of Accountancy on Young](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2026/mar/court-determines-taxpayer-lacked-profit-motive/); [The Tax Adviser on Huff](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2022/mar/prior-business-success-credence-donkey-breeders-profit-motive/); checked 2026-10-22). A dated written plan before year end, a separate bank account and card, a ledger, and a recorded unit-economics review each month are the cheap defenses. Economic substance is not a problem: renting from an unrelated cloud and selling tokens to unrelated customers at posted prices changes Josh's economic position and has a profit purpose; the 40 percent penalty tier applies only to undisclosed transactions, and a Schedule C with full books is disclosure ([26 USC 7701(o)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/7701); [26 USC 6662(i)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6662); checked 2026-10-22). The thing to avoid is circularity: do not let the "customer" be Josh or his employer, and do not pay related parties.

## Josh's follow-up questions, answered from the notes

**Do 8-GPU nodes and bigger models work better than single GPUs?** Yes, decisively, and for a reason that has nothing to do with hardware efficiency. The models that fit one H100 (gpt-oss-120b, Llama 3.3 70B, Qwen3 32B) have 10 to 22 hosts, price floors of $0.03 to $0.10 per million input tokens, and total OpenRouter pools of $1,000 to $85,000 a week; the model's best single-H100 cell is $0.98 per GPU-hour at 100 percent utilization against $2.30 of rent ([OpenRouter gpt-oss-120b endpoints](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/openai/gpt-oss-120b/endpoints); model output; checked 2026-10-22). The 600B to 770B MoE models need a whole 8x H200 or 8x B200 node, so only 4 or 5 hosts serve them, every host charges the lab's list price, and the pools are $3M to $8M a week. The node is the moat. Inside the node, Blackwell matters: at FP4 the B200 delivers 3.8 to 4.9 times H200 throughput on large MoE per InferenceX, so the same Step 5 endpoint earns $6.24 per H200-hour (48 percent breakeven) and $23.39 per B200-hour (15 percent breakeven) ([InferenceX R1 H200 vs B200](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/run/deepseek-r1-on-b200); model output; checked 2026-10-22). An 8x H100 node does not help because the 650 to 814 GB FP8 weights of Step 5 and Hy4 do not fit 640 GB; the models that do fit 8x H100 (Hy3, GLM 5.3 Flash, MiMo Flash) have floors too low to pay for the node (Plan D loses in every cell).

**Is a December prepayment really deductible?** Yes, under the conditions in the tax section: a fixed term of up to 12 months, service starting on or before January 1, 2027, full non-refundable payment in December, a business already open to the public, and total 2026 business losses (including any camper-van business) under $256,000. The regulation's Example 2 and Zaninovich are directly on point for a cash-basis taxpayer in the Ninth Circuit ([26 CFR 1.263(a)-4(f)(8)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subject-group-ECFR9b4e8d8fb4d5b2b/section-1.263(a)-4); [Zaninovich](https://openjurist.org/616/f2d/429); checked 2026-10-22). What the research did not find: any IRS guidance naming prepaid cloud compute, any published 12-month B200 price from a permissive provider (Vultr's "starting from $3.500" is a contact-sales pre-paid contract price with no term), and California's 2026 excess-business-loss threshold. The practical form is a prepaid Vultr HGX B200 node for N months (at $20,440 per node-month, $100,000 buys 4.9 months), invoiced and paid in December with a December start date.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "Should Josh rent GPUs and sell inference?" turns out to be "can Josh get a marketplace to route him list-price traffic for a model that needs a whole Blackwell node?" The GPU is not the scarce asset; the listing is. Every number that makes the plan attractive (a 15 percent breakeven, $54,607 after tax at 40 percent on $100,000) depends on two conventions that no contract protects: that four hosts keep charging Tencent's and StepFun's list prices, and that OpenRouter's router gives a new endpoint a few percent of a model's traffic. Both conventions are observable today and both can vanish in a week, which is exactly what happened to Step 5's volume. The tax code, by contrast, is stable and helpful: a short prepaid term paid in December, for a business that is already open, is deductible, and the $256,000 cap is the only hard ceiling. So the right posture is to treat the prepayment as a dial that is set on December 1 by what has actually happened, and to treat anything bought for its deduction alone as a loss with a 49.3 percent rebate.

The second shift is about what "small" means in this market. A single GPU is a hobby at today's floors; a single 8-GPU node is a real business at today's list prices; owning hardware is neither, because it combines a node's cost with a single GPU's demand and California's refusal to match the federal write-off. If Josh wants the business and not just the deduction, he should buy a benchmark week before he buys a quarter of a node-year, and he should keep the camper vans out of the same tax year.

## 1. Bottom line

Do not start a general GPU-rental or GPU-resale business, and do not buy servers for the deduction; both lose money in every modeled case, and relisting rented GPUs is barred by contract. Do start, in a staged way, an inference service on one rented 8x B200 node (Vultr, $3.50 per GPU-hour, terms permit a paid API) serving Step 5 Preview and Hy4 preview at 5 percent under their OpenRouter list-price floors, where the model shows $20.53 to $23.39 of conservative revenue per GPU-hour, breakeven at 15 to 17 percent utilization, and +$54,607 after tax over 12 months at 40 percent utilization on a $100,000 budget; test on a single Koyeb B200 in November, apply to OpenRouter and line up a direct customer now, and on December 1 size a non-refundable prepaid term (up to $100,000, kept under the $256,000 cap with any van purchase) to what is actually true, because OpenRouter's backlog is the one gate Josh cannot force, and the December paying customer will most likely be a direct one.
