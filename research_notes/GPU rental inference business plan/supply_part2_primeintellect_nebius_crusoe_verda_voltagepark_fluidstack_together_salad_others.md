# GPU rental supply, part 2: Prime Intellect, Nebius, Crusoe, Verda, Voltage Park, FluidStack, Together AI, Salad, and other GPU clouds (prices and terms for a US buyer running AI inference)

All numbers below were read live on 2026-10-22 unless a line says otherwise. "OD" = on-demand. "GPU-hr" = per GPU per hour. Prices are USD and exclude tax unless noted. Where a provider only shows a whole-node price, the per-GPU figure is the node price divided by the GPU count and is marked "derived". "Sales call" means the provider shows no public number. "Not found" means I looked and found no number. Third-party tracker numbers are labeled as such and are not provider prices.

Method note: pages were read with the crw scraper (live fetch, cache disabled). The built-in web search budget ran out partway through, so a few secondary items (Scaleway H100 prices, Massed Compute ToS body, Cudo ToS, OVH ToS, Radiant/Ori prices) are recorded as gaps rather than guessed.

## Key question 1: What is the cheapest reliable H100 SXM, H200 and B200 per hour on-demand today, and at 1, 3 and 12-month terms, across these providers?

### Takeaway
Among providers with a published rate card, the cheapest on-demand H100 SXM today is Massed Compute at $3.14/GPU-hr (1x or 8x, same price), with GMI Cloud advertising "from $2.60" and Voltage Park "starting at $1.99" (both without a full rate card). The cheapest published H200 is GMI "from $3.20", then Massed Compute $3.62 (NVL) and Crusoe $4.29 (HGX). The cheapest published B200 is GMI "from $5.00" (limited availability), then Massed Compute $5.43 (8x node only), Lium $6.00 (one pod), Together $8.19 (cluster, 8+ GPUs), Verda $8.27 and Nebius $8.50. Only Verda, Jarvislabs, DigitalOcean, Latitude and Together publish term discounts without a sales call; most other reserved prices are "sales call".

## Key question 2: Which providers explicitly permit or forbid running a public inference API on rented GPUs?

### Takeaway
Only Salad, Prime Intellect and DigitalOcean say in writing that a customer may build and sell its own services on the rented compute. Voltage Park, Crusoe, Together, FluidStack, Jarvislabs, GMI Cloud and Massed Compute forbid reselling, sublicensing, service-bureau or timesharing use and are otherwise silent on a customer's own paid API. CoreWeave, Hot Aisle, Verda, Nebius and Lium forbid transferring rights or access but contemplate "end users" and are silent on paid APIs. Hetzner expressly allows granting third parties use of the server.

### Inferences
- A business whose product is a public inference API (customers pay per token) is on the safest contractual footing at Salad, Prime Intellect, DigitalOcean and Hetzner, because those terms name "your own applications, APIs" or "your own products and services" or "third parties ... use" as allowed.
- At Crusoe, Together, CoreWeave, Hot Aisle, Nebius and Verda the terms do not mention a customer-operated API either way; they prohibit transferring or reselling "the Services" (the provider's platform), and they define end users or customers, which implies that hosting an application used by third parties is contemplated. A written confirmation from the provider would remove doubt.
- Voltage Park's "internal business purposes" license, FluidStack's "service bureau facilities" ban, Jarvislabs' "only ... for the purpose of Deep learning", and GMI's Exhibit A are the most restrictive texts; a paid public API arguably conflicts with "internal business purposes" and GMI's "make the Services or GPU capacity available to any third party" absent approval.

### Gaps
- Massed Compute's master Terms & Conditions page rendered only navigation in every scraper (chrome, camoufox, impersonated-http, links mode) and WebFetch; only the EULA could be read.
- GMI's Acceptable Use Policy URL returned model-documentation content instead of the policy (site bug on 2026-10-22).
- Verda's term "Accepted Purposes" is used in §3.1 but its definition was not present in the scraped text.
- Crusoe's separate Acceptable Use Policy text was not in the legal page markdown (only the ToS was); the ToS Restrictions clause is quoted instead.
- Latitude (Megaport GSA), Cudo, OVH, Scaleway and Radiant terms were not reviewed.

### Inferences
- The phrase "use the Services to power your own products and services" (Prime Intellect) and "sell your own applications, APIs, and inference services" (Salad) are the clearest green lights for a paid inference API.
- "Internal business purposes" (Voltage Park) and "service bureau" / "timesharing" bans (Voltage Park, FluidStack, Prime Intellect) are the clauses most likely to be read against serving third-party API traffic; Prime Intellect resolves the tension with its express exception, Voltage Park and FluidStack do not.
- GMI's Exhibit A reaches beyond resale to "provide remote access to ... GPU capacity", which a public API could be argued to do; written approval is the safe route.

### Gaps
- Massed Compute master T&C, GMI AUP, Crusoe AUP body, Verda "Accepted Purposes" definition, Latitude/Megaport GSA, Cudo, OVH, Scaleway and Radiant terms were not captured.
- No provider page read defines whether a customer's public inference API counts as "resale" or "service bureau"; that is a legal interpretation question for counsel.
