Bottom line

Worth doing, but only in one shape. Rent one 8-GPU B200 node, serve the handful of big open-weight models that still sell at list price on OpenRouter, and get listed there. In that shape the node clears its rent at about 40% utilization on conservative throughput, and at 20% on typical throughput. The idea as first framed, single H100s serving gpt-oss-120b, Llama 3.3 70B and Qwen3 32B, loses about $7,000 a month for every $100,000 of annual budget, because those models' entire OpenRouter pools are $1,000 to $85,000 a week split among 10 to 21 providers at floor prices. The tax deduction is real and large (a $100,000 non-refundable prepay in December saves about $45,000 to $49,000 of 2026 tax, a $250,000 prepay about $104,000 to $117,000) but it only halves a loss; it does not turn a losing shape into a winning one. The two things that decide the outcome are outside the spreadsheet: whether OpenRouter accepts the listing (it reportedly accepts about one of forty applications a day and prioritizes providers that fill gaps) and whether the thin-host preview models keep their price and volume. So the plan is staged: test on month-to-month GPUs in November, apply to OpenRouter in week one, and only prepay in the last week of December if real traffic shows up. The prepay still counts for 2026 even if the term starts January 1.

What has to be true

  • OpenRouter lists you. Applications are reviewed on a rolling basis with "a large backlog" and priority to proprietary models and gap-fillers. One secondhand account puts intake at about 40 a day with about 1 accepted. Hy4-preview with 4 hosts is a credible gap. Nothing in the research found a solo provider who got listed, or one who was refused.
  • The preview models hold. Hy4-preview's card says "early version... known issues"; Step 5 Preview lost 88% of its volume in a week. If Tencent cuts its list price, the four pegged hosts will follow and the margin shrinks.
  • Throughput is real. Every Hy4, Step 5 and GLM 5.3 throughput cell is mapped from DeepSeek-class benchmarks (8x B200: 1,500 output tokens/s per GPU conservative, 3,000 typical). Run vllm bench serve on the actual node in week three before pricing anything.
  • The prepay has a business purpose. Vultr's prepaid contract is the same $3.50 as on-demand, so the reason to prepay is guaranteed capacity (B200 nodes are gated and 2026 prices rose 10% to 40%), not a discount. Koyeb quotes reservations "up to 50% off" by contact; get that quote in writing. A prepay that exists only for the deduction fails economic substance.

A. Supply: where to rent, and the price

Thirty providers were read. No one publishes a 12-month price for H100, H200 or B200; every reserved term is a sales call or a request for quote, except DigitalOcean (12-month H100 $3.26, H200 $3.40, both above the cheapest on-demand rate elsewhere), Verda (2%, 3%, 8% off for 1, 3, 12 months), Jarvislabs (2%, 3%, 9%), Together (prepaid up to 6 months) and Vast (self-serve prepay discount of up to 50% on an existing instance). Prices rose in October 2026: Nebius lifted H100 from $3.85 to $4.50 and B200 from $7.15 to $8.50 on October 1, citing HBM supply; Jarvislabs went from $2.69 to $3.49 on October 5. The cheapest sources with terms that allow a paid API are Vultr (8-GPU plans: H100 $2.30, B200 $3.50, MI300X $1.85, MI355X $2.59), Koyeb (single GPUs: H100 $2.50, H200 $3.00, B200 $5.50, RTX PRO 6000 $2.20) and DigitalOcean (the most explicit terms: "via resale, your own products and services, or public-facing websites").

Terms of service matter more than price. Voltage Park, Crusoe, Together, FluidStack, Jarvislabs, GMI, Massed Compute, TensorDock and Shadeform bar resale or "service bureau" use and are silent on your own API, so get written consent before serving third parties there. Lambda's restriction is on reselling its platform, not on serving customers. Vast's September 2026 terms assume "end users of the Services" and Vast markets a Serverless product for inference, so a paid API is permitted. RunPod's main terms acknowledge "your end users" but reference supplemental terms nobody can find, so it is ambiguous.

B. Demand: "Airbnb for inference" marketplaces

OpenRouter is the only marketplace that is simultaneously open to outside providers, large (about 22 trillion tokens a day; the top open model carries 25.7 trillion a week), and pays USD. You set your own per-token price; OpenRouter bills users at your list price, takes no cut from providers, and pays by monthly invoice. Requirements: an OpenAI-compatible chat completions endpoint with streaming and usage counts, a models feed in OpenRouter's schema, monthly invoicing, and a published privacy policy that says whether prompts are logged or used for training. Routing is price-weighted but uptime, time to first token and throughput are measured per endpoint; endpoints under 95% uptime get less traffic and under 80% become fallback only. The cheapest endpoint does not win by itself: on gpt-oss-120b the two floor-priced endpoints carried 0.8% and 7.2% of one day's tokens while a provider at four times the floor carried 18.5%.

Everything else is thin. Hugging Face Inference Providers has a published nine-step path but requires a paid Team or Enterprise plan and its registration thread has had no staff reply since September 2025. NanoGPT takes providers by email and says a single-GPU or single-operator service is unlikely to fit. Requesty, Unify, Martian, AIML API, Featherless and Parasail have no provider program; Kluster.ai shut down; cheapinference.com is a parked domain (cheaperinference.com exists but routes other people's cloud API keys, not GPUs). Chutes, Targon and Gonka are Bittensor networks paid in tokens and need TEE-capable bare metal, which excludes RunPod and Vast. The gateways (Vercel, Cloudflare, Portkey, Helicone, LiteLLM) route to fixed provider lists and pay nobody.

OpenRouter: open-weight models, providers, prices and volume

The models most people name (gpt-oss-120b, Llama 3.3 70B, Qwen3 32B, DeepSeek R1) are now small pools with many hosts. The volume sits on a new generation: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (27.5T tokens a week, 29 hosts, floor $0.07/$0.18), GLM 5.3 Flash (12.4T, 25 hosts), Tencent Hy4-preview (9.7T, 4 hosts, all at $0.834/$2.501), MiMo V2.6 Flash (9.1T, 8 hosts), GLM 5.3 (5.7T, 36 hosts, median output $4.30), Hy3 (3.5T, 5 hosts), Step 5 Preview (3.0T, 4 hosts, all at $1.00/$2.70), Kimi K3 (2.0T, 20 hosts, median output $14). The opening is the ratio of tokens to hosts at a price that pays for a B200.

C. Backup: "Airbnb for GPU time" (reselling GPU hours)

Re-listing a rented GPU on Vast.ai or a similar market is blocked on both ends. Every rental contract read bans resale (Lambda, Nebius, Crusoe, Voltage Park, Hyperbolic, TensorDock, Vultr "without our written permission"), and Vast's Standard Hosting Agreement makes the host warrant that "it is the sole owner of the Hardware" located "at the Provider". Technically a root VM could run Vast's Docker-based host daemon (the installer does not check for bare metal), but RunPod pods cannot and TensorDock hosting needs bare metal. The money is not there either: Vast's implied host share is about 80% (its blog says live prices run about 25% above host earnings), so an H100 host takes about $2.02 an hour against a $1.68 to $2.30 rental, a spread of $0 to $0.34 at 100% utilization and negative at the calculator's default 65%. RunPod closed its Community Cloud host program to new hosts on February 24, 2026.

The one legitimate resale route is SF Compute: anyone who buys reserved node-hours there can resell unused hours on the same order book (minimum one 8-GPU node for one minute, paid in platform credits, seller-side fee unpublished, average realized discount 25%). That is a hedge for a reservation you no longer need, not a revenue line. The model's scenarios 3a, 3b and 5 keep the Vast numbers for comparison, flagged as blocked for rented hardware; scenario 4b shows Vast with owned hardware, which is allowed.

D. Unit economics: revenue per GPU-hour versus rent

Revenue per GPU-hour = output tokens per second per GPU (independent benchmarks, conservative and typical) x 3,600 x the price you post (5% under today's OpenRouter floor) x utilization, with input tokens billed at 3 prompt tokens per completion token and a 20% prefill haircut on throughput. Pick a GPU below. On the B200, Hy4-preview earns about $20 an hour at full utilization against $3.50 of rent, so it breaks even at 17% utilization; GLM 5.3 breaks even at 52%; DeepSeek V4.1 Flash never does at the floor price because 29 hosts have pushed it to $0.07/$0.18. On an H100, nothing in the mid-size tier earns more than $0.65 an hour at 70% utilization against $2.30 of rent.

F. Tax mechanics

You are a cash-basis sole proprietor, so GPU bills are deductible when paid (Reg. 1.461-1(a)(1); Pub 538). A prepayment is the exception: it is deductible when paid only if the benefit ends by the earlier of 12 months after it starts or the end of the next tax year (Reg. 1.263(a)-4(f), the 12-month rule). The Ninth Circuit, which covers California, applied the one-year rule to a cash-basis taxpayer in Zaninovich v. Commissioner (616 F.2d 429, 1980). So a reservation paid in full and non-refundable in December 2026 qualifies whether it runs December 2026 to November 2027 or January to December 2027; one that runs into 2028 does not. The regulation's examples warn against fixed-quantity blocks of hours and auto-renewing contracts, so sign a term for specific GPUs and dates, not a bucket of credits.

The business must already be open to the public when you pay. Under IRC 195 and Kellett (T.C. Memo. 2022-62), an online business begins when it is open to customers even with zero revenue; if the API is not open until January, every 2026 dollar, including the prepay, becomes a start-up cost amortized over 180 months. Only the net loss offsets W-2 pay, and IRC 461(l) caps it at $256,000 for a single filer in 2026 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32), with the excess carried forward as an NOL; California has its own cap ($313,000 in 2025, 2026 not yet published). Material participation is the "substantially all" test or 500 hours (Reg. 1.469-5T); keep a log. The hobby-loss factors (Reg. 1.183-2(b)) and economic substance (IRC 7701(o), 20% to 40% penalties under 6662) are satisfied by a real service sold at market prices with a written plan and books, which is what Huff (allowed) had and Young (denied, $16M of other income, plan written after the audit began) did not. Owned hardware gets 100% federal bonus depreciation if placed in service by December 31 (IRC 168(k) as amended by P.L. 119-21), but California allows only $25,000 of Section 179 (phased out above $200,000 of purchases) plus 5% first-year MACRS for a December in-service date. The arithmetic checks: $100,000 of expense saves $49,300; a $100,000 server saves $40,536.

Alternatives: AMD, TPUs, Macs, buying hardware, crypto, other platforms

Non-NVIDIA and value-tier accelerators

AMD is the one alternative that changes the answer. Vultr sells 8x MI355X at $2.59 per GPU-hour and 8x MI300X at $1.85 (and 8x B200 at $3.50, which is why B200 is the baseline). On InferenceX the MI355X delivers 87% of a B200's gpt-oss-120b throughput and matches it on cost per token, but on DeepSeek-class MoE models its FP8 path is only 35% of B200 (90% on the tuned FP4 path at high per-user speeds), and no public MI355X number exists for Hy4, GLM 5.3 or Kimi K3. The model therefore scores an MI355X node below B200 on conservative numbers and roughly equal on typical ones; a two-day benchmark settles it. MI300X is cheap but its MLA path is slow (26% of H200 on DeepSeek R1), so it loses money on the big models. Google TPU v7 costs $12 a chip-hour and v6e needs a quota request; AWS Trainium has only batch-1 data for these models; Gaudi 3 has no on-demand listing; Qualcomm, Tenstorrent, Groq, Cerebras, SambaNova and the newer ASIC startups are not rentable as raw hardware by a small customer. The RTX PRO 6000 (96 GB, Koyeb $2.20, RunPod $2.49) is the best value for 70B to 120B models, but those models do not pay.

Macs and unified-memory appliances

Not for this. A Mac Studio serves big MoE models at batch 1 to 4 (DeepSeek V3.2 4-bit: 19 tokens/s single, 26 at batch 2; Kimi K2.6: 21 to 41; gpt-oss-120b: 68 single, 187 at batch 8) and its prompt processing is 200 to 700 tokens/s, so time to first token is 5 to 48 seconds on 1k to 8k prompts, which OpenRouter publishes and routes away from. On cost per output token it loses by about 10x on gpt-oss-120b and 25x on 70B dense models versus an H100, and only lands in the same band on DeepSeek-class models at low per-user speed. Four M3 Ultras over Thunderbolt 5 reach 32 tokens/s on DeepSeek V3.1, still batch 1. The M5 Ultra 512 GB price was unannounced on October 22; no host offers a 512 GB Mac; MacStadium's policy allows third-party access only inside a SaaS offering. A DGX Spark has far better prefill (1,700 to 6,000 tokens/s) but tops out around 175 aggregate tokens/s on gpt-oss-120b. The tax angle (100% federal bonus, $25k California Section 179) is real but small relative to the throughput gap.

Colocation that accepts shipped gear with printed prices: Psychz (Dallas/Ashburn) 4.99 kW cabinet $1,058 a month on a 1-year term, MillionMiner (Nebraska/Missouri/Mississippi) $0.075 per kWh all-in and month-to-month, Hurricane Electric Fremont $600 a month for a cabinet. The model's owned RTX PRO 6000 scenario earns only $0.28 per GPU-hour on mid-size models at 40% utilization: after a $40,000 federal-plus-California tax saving on a $100,000 purchase, the 12-month after-tax cash is about -$58,000, versus +$55,000 for the prepaid B200 node. An 8-card box (768 GB) could in principle hold Hy3 and GLM 5.3, but there is no benchmark of PCIe-only tensor parallel on those models, so owning is a 2027 question once a measured number exists.

Rental price trend

H100 rent fell from over $8 an hour in 2023 to a $2.81 composite in December 2025 and a $1.70 one-year contract low in October 2025, then reversed: $2.35 contracts by March 2026 and October 2026 indices of $2.88 to $3.75 for on-demand, up 10% to 26% year over year. H200 moved from $4.42 to $5.88 on one index between August and October 2026. The only 2027 forecasts come from market blogs ($1 to $1.50 as Blackwell saturates) and one analyst note on HBM prices doubling. Price rises are the business reason to lock a term; price falls are the reason to keep the term short.

Crypto and token-paid GPU networks (flagged, low priority)

Nothing token-paid is profitable on rented GPUs, and most networks forbid rented or virtual hardware anyway (Chutes and Targon need TEE-capable bare metal and explicitly exclude RunPod and Vast; Salad needs an owned Windows PC; Aethir needs bare metal with a large stake). Classic proof-of-work mining is dead except one fresh coin (Pearl) that pays an RTX 4090 about $9.84 a day on WhatToMine at $0.10 per kWh, listed on a single exchange with no market cap; every legacy coin pays $0.06 to $1.06 a day. The live "AI mining" rates: Lium (Bittensor subnet 51) publishes measured provider earnings of $1,292 per H100-month and $10,333 per 8x H100 node-month, 95% to the provider, paid daily in USDT or subnet alpha; Targon (subnet 4) caps H200 at $2.90 to $3.50 per card-hour on TEE nodes; Akash prices an H100 at $2.73 an hour with unknown utilization. Projection for November 1 to December 31 on rented hardware: a 4090 loses about $660 at typical rental rates and an H100 loses $200 to $3,200; on owned hardware at California power a 4090 nets about $465 on Lium and an H100 about $2,300, and an owned 8x H200 TEE node on Targon could net $31,000 to $38,000 in subnet alpha if it wins auctions. Token rewards are ordinary income at receipt (Notice 2014-21, Rev. Rul. 2023-14), so they do not help the W-2 offset. Most rental contracts (Lambda, Nebius, Voltage Park, TensorDock, Vast for card-funded accounts) ban mining outright.

Other platforms and monetization routes

Beyond OpenRouter and your own API, the routes that could produce a paying customer by December 31 are: managed-inference consulting where the client holds the cloud account and you charge a fee (Upwork demand exists, and it sidesteps every resale clause); dedicated endpoints for startups priced under the incumbents (Modal $3.95, fal $2.49 to $4.50, Together $5.49, Baseten $6.50, Fireworks $8.00 per H100-hour; B200 $6.25 to $13.00), which means a small provider wins at $2.00 to $3.50; and SF Compute as a hedge for unused reserved hours. Startup credit programs are mostly closed to a sole proprietorship (AWS Activate Founders $1,000 to $5,000, Nebius $5,000 and Modal $30 a month are the exceptions). Referral programs pay in credits. NVIDIA DGX Cloud Lepton supply is partner-only; Compute Exchange is a parked domain; Hydra Host's partner program has no published share.

"Airbnb for GPU time" marketplaces, best first

  1. SF Compute. Resell reserved node-hours you bought there, on the same order book, in USD credits. The only resale route a rental contract allows.
  2. Vast.ai host. Owned hardware only; about 80% host share; PayPal, Stripe or Wise weekly; H100 median $2.53, 4090 $0.67, 5090 $0.87 to renters.
