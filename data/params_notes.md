# params.json: every chosen number, its source, and the reason

Filled on 2026-10-22 from the research notes in `research_notes/GPU rental inference business plan/` only (supply parts 1 and 2, OpenRouter notes and raw JSON, throughput benchmarks, resale marketplaces, owned hardware and colo, tax and licenses, alternative accelerators, in-stock hardware). No new web research was done. Abbreviations: OD = on-demand; GPU-hr = per GPU per hour; m1/m3/m12 = month-to-month, 3-month prepaid, 12-month prepaid rate.

How the file was built:
- `data/params.json` non-OpenRouter cells were generated from the notes by a one-off builder script kept outside the repo in the session scratchpad: `C:\Users\josh\AppData\Local\Temp\claude\C--Users-josh-code-gpu-house\bfdabbc6-f05f-4a32-b67a-ae912b8841dd\scratchpad\build_params.py`.
- OpenRouter cells were written by `python -I model/fill_openrouter.py` from the raw endpoints JSON beside the notes.
- Model runs: `python -I model/model.py` (conservative, writes `data/results.json` and `data/results.md`) and `$env:CASE="typical"; python -I model/model.py` (writes `data/results_typical.json` and `data/results_typical.md`).

Provider rule used for rates (final, after the gap-closing note): a rate is taken only from a provider whose terms were read and either expressly permit a public paid inference API or contain no clause that forbids it. The gap-closing note verified Vultr, Koyeb, DigitalOcean, Vast and Lambda; earlier notes verified Nebius, Verda, CoreWeave, Hot Aisle, Salad, Prime Intellect, Hetzner, Hyperbolic and Lium as silent or permissive. RunPod is treated as ambiguous (its Compute Services Supplemental Terms could not be found) and is shown for comparison only. Providers whose terms forbid resale or service-bureau use (Voltage Park, Crusoe, Together, FluidStack, Jarvislabs, GMI, Massed Compute, TensorDock, Shadeform and the clouds listed only through Shadeform) and providers whose terms were never read (Cirrascale, Daytona, TensorWave, Oracle, Azure, OVH, Latitude) appear only in notes as alternatives. Cheapest permissive source per GPU is the gap-closing note's final table. Three term rules: m1 = cheapest permissive on-demand rate; m3 = a published 3-month price from a permissive provider if it is below on-demand, else m1 x 0.95 (estimate); m12 = DigitalOcean's published 12-month reserved price, unless it is above the cheapest permissive on-demand price, in which case the prepaid-contract provider's cheaper price is used (Vultr bare-metal "starting from" pre-paid contract prices, or the on-demand rate as the ceiling for a contact-only reservation), because no rational buyer prepays more than on-demand. Each GPU also carries a `test_rate` (single-GPU Koyeb or Lambda price) for the month-to-month test phase, because Vultr's cheaper 8-GPU plans need a manually reviewed account limit; model.py does not read `test_rate` yet (staged scenarios 6a and 6b use m1).

## 3. OpenRouter cells (USD per million tokens)

Method (`model/fill_openrouter.py`): each endpoint priced at $0 is dropped, and each endpoint whose `status` is not 0 is dropped (the API marks degraded or disabled endpoints with -2 and -5; the notes do not document the codes). Providers = distinct `provider_name` values left. Min and median are over providers, using each provider's cheapest endpoint (by output price, then input). `sum_inv_sq_price` = sum over the remaining endpoints of 1 / (out + 3 x in)^2. Weekly tokens are the rounded "Tokens processed in the last 7 days" card values in `weekly_tokens.json` (3 significant figures, includes the in-progress day). Source for every row: `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/{slug}/endpoints`, raw copy beside the notes, checked 2026-10-22.

## 4. Throughput (output tokens per second per GPU; conservative / typical; ratio_in = 1.0 for every row)

Both planning columns come from the throughput notes' "Recommended planning numbers" table. Conservative = mixed real traffic at about 50 tok/s per user with a general-purpose engine (about one third of the vendor offline figure for single-GPU models, about one quarter for 8-GPU MoE models). Typical = tuned engine, 30 to 50 tok/s per user, 1k/1k-like traffic (about half to two thirds of vendor offline). ratio_in is 1.0 because the planning numbers are for 1k/1k-like shapes (ShareGPT averages about 215 in / 196 out). Vendor offline 8k/1k figures were not used.

## 7. Caveats

- Vultr drives the H100, B200, MI300X and MI355X rates (Plans A, C, D, E, F and the B200 unit economics). Its terms permit a paid API (gap-closing note), but it sells these GPUs only as 8-GPU plans, publishes no stock indicator (MI355X was out of stock on getdeploying Oct 22), and gates new accounts behind a manually reviewed account limit ("consistent usage and a positive billing history"; reviewers describe a 30-day "normal usage" wait and a GPU approval gauntlet). The month-to-month TEST phase (scenarios 6a/6b, Nov 15 to Dec 31) should therefore be priced at the single-GPU test_rate (Koyeb H100 $2.50, H200 $3.00, B200 $5.50, RTX PRO 6000 $2.20; DigitalOcean MI300X $2.59; no 1x MI355X source), not at the Vultr m1; model.py does not read test_rate yet.
- The m12 rates for H100 ($2.30), B200 ($3.50) and MI300X ($1.75) are Vultr bare-metal "starting from" prices "for pre-paid contract terms" with no published term length or discount, used because DigitalOcean's published 12-month prices ($3.26, none, $1.91) sit above the cheapest on-demand rate and no rational buyer prepays more than on-demand. H200 ($3.00), MI355X ($2.59) and RTX PRO 6000 ($2.20) m12 are the on-demand rate used as a ceiling for a contact-only reservation (Koyeb "up to 50% off", Vultr prepaid "from $2.65" above on-demand). So the 12-month prepaid scenarios (2b, 6b) model the tax timing of a prepay at roughly the on-demand rate; any negotiated discount is upside. Vast reserved (H100 m3 $1.77) is a host-locked prepay on a marketplace instance.
- RunPod (Community H100 $2.69, H200 $3.59, B200 $5.98, RTX PRO 6000 $1.69, 5090 $0.69) is cheaper than several chosen rates but its Compute Services Supplemental Terms could not be found, so it is treated as ambiguous and excluded from values. Cirrascale (B200 annual $4.79, MI300X 3-month $3.66, RTX PRO 6000 annual $1.37), Daytona, TensorWave, Oracle, Azure, OVH and Latitude terms were never read.
- The opportunity ranking sorts by margin against m12, which now equals m1 for every GPU except H100 (m12 = m1 = Vultr) and MI300X ($1.75 vs $1.85); MI355X m12 is an estimate while B200 m12 is a published "starting from". Compare rev_per_gpu_hour_100 and breakeven_util_m1 when rows are close.
- The Vast resale scenarios (3a, 3b, 5) assume you can list rented GPUs on Vast. The Vast Standard Hosting Agreement requires the host to be the sole owner of the hardware and the hardware to be located at the host, so those scenarios only hold for owned servers (scenario 4 hardware and Plan G), not for rented instances. The 0.20 host fee is inferred from one blog sentence, not published. Vast's renter terms are verified permissive for a paid API (the service-bureau clause is tied to competing with Vast).
- Every AMD throughput cell is an estimate built from three InferenceX model pages; the only measured AMD models are gpt-oss-120b, Llama 3.3 70B and DeepSeek R1. MI300X is a poor fit for MLA models (DeepSeek class, 0.26x H200), so Plan B's models on MI300X are not modeled as a plan; GLM-class MoE on AMD has no measurement at all. The MI355X conservative-to-typical gap on DeepSeek-class models (500 vs 2,700) reflects that the tuned FP4 config was only run at 75 tok/s per user and above.
- The conservative and typical throughput numbers for the models that matter most for revenue (Hy4 preview, Step 5, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, GLM 5.3, GLM 5.3 Flash, MiMo, Mistral Small 4) are mappings from other models, and for three of them the notes do not record the parameter count. Kimi K3 cannot be modeled on one node at all. A `vllm bench serve` run on the target hardware should replace every mapped row before pricing is set.
- Typical-case revenue per GPU-hour for Step 5 Preview and Hy4 preview on B200 (and MI355X) reaches $31 to $38 at 100% utilization and current list prices. That is arithmetic (2,700 to 3,000 output tok/s per GPU x about $3.50 per million blended), not a unit error, but it depends on an optimistic DeepSeek-class number (46% of the vendor offline figure) and on list prices that four resellers currently hold; Step 5 traffic fell 88% in a week.
- The RTX PRO 6000 box (Plan G) is priced from Bizon's chassis base price plus Saitech's special-order card price; Bizon's configured total, freight and the $500 colo setup are not included. Six Server Edition cards draw more than a 30A/208V cabinet's continuous rating, so the Psychz $1,058 plan technically fits a 4-card or Max-Q build; MillionMiner's per-kWh plan ($235 per month) is the note's match for the 6-card box. Its throughput cells come from one single-card vLLM run and TensorRT-LLM FP4 pipeline-parallel tables; no multi-GPU vLLM measurement exists.
- OpenRouter prices and volumes are a one-day snapshot (2026-10-22); V4.1 Flash volume fell 31% week over week and snapshots replace each other within weeks. Weekly tokens are rounded page values. The 1/p^2 routing-share assumption in the model is not verified by the notes; on gpt-oss-120b the two floor-price endpoints carried only 0.8% and 7.2% of the day's tokens. OpenRouter pays providers by monthly invoicing (no net term published), requires 95%+ uptime for normal routing, and says it has "a large backlog" of provider applications and prioritizes proprietary models, so listing time and cash float (30 to 60 days) are open risks.
- The excess business loss cap is the federal single-filer figure; California's own 2026 threshold is unpublished (2025: $313,000) and the model applies the federal cap to the California computation. California's $25,000 Section 179 cap phases out above $200,000 of property placed in service, which the model does not apply (relevant at the $250,000 spend level).
- Owned-hardware colo opex for H100/H200/B200 is a broker ballpark; no named Texas, Tennessee or Reno provider publishes a GPU-rack rate card. The used 8x H100 price is a single refurbished listing (out of stock on Oct 22, orders over $50K through sales). The spend levels ($50K to $250K) buy less than one 8-GPU node for 12 months on H200, B200 or MI355X, so the node plans show fractional GPUs.
- The results.md unit-economics table only covers H100, H200 and B200 (model.py hard-codes those keys); MI300X, MI355X and RTX PRO 6000 unit economics appear only in the opportunities list in results.json and in the Plan E, F and G scenario blocks.
- H100 PCIe and MI300X spot and the MI355X test_rate are null (no terms-verified source). The spot column is not used by the model.
